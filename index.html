About

I study how yeasts gain and lose the ability to use different sugars, using comparative genomics and machine learning across more than a thousand yeast strains.

My current focus is xylose: why a few wild yeasts use it better than industry-engineered strains, and how to turn them into better chassis for biofuel production. Before Madison, I trained as a plant breeder and quantitative geneticist working on West African crops.

Research

  • Machine learning across yeasts

    Linking gene-family content to growth on different carbon sources, and tracing predictions back to the genes that drive them.

  • Phylogenomics

    How carbon-metabolism gene families were gained, lost, and modified across the yeast subphylum.

  • HXT & PMI40 genetics

    CRISPR-Cas9 and growth assays testing how sugar transporters and mannose metabolism shape what a cell can eat.

  • Quantitative genetics

    Earlier work on maize heterotic groups, genomic prediction, and selection in West African crops.
